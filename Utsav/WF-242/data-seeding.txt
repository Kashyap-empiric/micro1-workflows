"GRAFTON INCIDENT - RECORD. Fictional, for testing. Corviston Software Pvt Ltd. Incident name:
Grafton. Opened by the security team, handed to GRC for the notification position. All times IST.

WHAT HAPPENED. A long-lived service account token for our build system leaked inside a public
container image layer that was pushed by mistake on 6 July 2026. On the night of Saturday 11 July an
attacker used that token to authenticate to the self-hosted CI runner ci-runner-04 from an
unrecognised network, then moved from there to the internal file host fs-03 and to the configuration
vault vault-prod-02. The attacker read files on fs-03 and read the secret inventory on vault-prod-02.
There is no evidence of any write, deletion or encryption by the attacker, and no evidence of any
activity after 03:00 on Sunday 12 July.

TIMELINE.

Mon 6 July 2026, 15:22 - the container image carrying the token is pushed to a public registry. Not
noticed at the time.

Sat 11 July 2026, 21:14 - FIRST ALERT. EDR on ci-runner-04 raises anomalous credential use from an
unrecognised ASN. The on-call engineer acknowledges it, reads it as noise from a vendor scan window,
and closes it without escalating. Nothing is known at this point about what was reached, or about
whether any personal data is involved.

Sat 11 July 2026, 21:31 to Sun 12 July 2026, 03:00 - attacker activity. Pivot from ci-runner-04 to
fs-03 at 00:35. Directory reads on fs-03 from 00:48 to 02:05. Secret inventory read on vault-prod-02
at 01:12. Last observed activity 03:00.

Sun 12 July 2026, 08:30 - a contractor reports that her work laptop, asset CS-LAP-0219, was stolen
from a car in Berlin on the evening of Saturday 11 July. Logged as a device-loss report of its own.
Folded into Grafton later that day because one of the stores in the Grafton review, hr-laptop-backup,
also had a copy sitting on that device. Forensics found no link between the theft and the intrusion:
the leaked token is the only entry vector, and CS-LAP-0219 has not authenticated to anything since
the theft.

Sun 12 July 2026, 11:42 - MDM confirms the remote wipe on CS-LAP-0219 executed. The device console
shows no successful pre-boot authentication after the theft. The key vault shows the escrowed
recovery key for that device was never checked out. The whole-volume encryption key for CS-LAP-0219
was not held on ci-runner-04, fs-03 or vault-prod-02, and was not in anything the attacker reached.

Sun 12 July 2026, 19:20 - a second EDR alert fires on fs-03 for a bulk read. On-call escalates and
the incident Grafton is opened. At this point we know a host was read from. Nobody has yet
established what was in the files, or whether any of it is personal data.

Mon 13 July 2026, 09:40 - Thornbury IR, our forensics vendor, delivers its report. The file-level
and secret-level findings set out under BLAST RADIUS below are that report.

Mon 13 July 2026, 14:00 - CONTAINMENT. Token revoked. ci-runner-04 and fs-03 rebuilt from clean
images. Every secret on vault-prod-02 rotated. Egress to the attacker's ranges blocked. Contained.

Tue 14 July 2026, 07:00 - GRC picks the notification position up. This record is handed over as it
stands.

BLAST RADIUS.

Reached by the attacker: ci-runner-04 (build runner, entry point), fs-03 (internal file host),
vault-prod-02 (configuration vault).

Read on vault-prod-02: the full secret inventory, at 01:12 on Sunday 12 July. Among the secrets read
is analytics-pepper-v3, the pseudonymisation pepper used by the analytics pipeline. Every secret on
vault-prod-02 was rotated at containment.

Read on fs-03, by path and time on Sunday 12 July: /srv/analytics/events/ at 00:48,
/srv/customers/acme/crm-mirror/ at 01:30, /srv/finance/payroll/sg/ at 01:47,
/srv/finance/contractors/br/ at 01:58, /srv/marketing/drafts/ at 02:05. Nothing else on fs-03 was
opened.

Not in the blast radius: the production customer database cluster (separate VPC, no route from fs-03,
no credential for it on any host the attacker reached), the corporate Google Workspace tenant, and
the source code hosting.

Separate limb: CS-LAP-0219, the stolen laptop. It is not a host on our network and the attacker
never touched it. It is in the Grafton review only because one store had a copy on it.

WHAT WE STILL DO NOT KNOW. Whether the attacker copied out what they read or only read it. The
egress logs on fs-03 cover the window and the volumes are consistent with either. Treat the reads as
reads that happened."
