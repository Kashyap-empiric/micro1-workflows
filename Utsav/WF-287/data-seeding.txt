2) Make a Google Doc called "Research Cost Rules Pack - 2025 Return" in my Google Drive, with
exactly this text:

"RESEARCH COST RULES PACK, 2025 RETURN. Pulled for the Vantridge return. Fictional company,
fictional rules, invented for testing. The rule numbers below are made up and are not citations to
anything. The rules and the wording below are the authoritative and complete rulebook for this
exercise. Treat them as correct, and do not rely on outside knowledge of any real tax code, any
real revenue procedure, any real regulation or any real agency notice. If it is not in this pack or
in the circular, it does not apply here.

RULE 400. DOMESTIC RESEARCH EXPENDITURE, AND THE DEDUCTION.

400.1 A research expenditure is an amount paid or incurred in connection with the taxpayer's trade
or business which represents a research or experimental cost in the experimental or laboratory
sense. A cost is in that sense where it is for activity intended to discover information that would
eliminate uncertainty about the development or improvement of a product, and uncertainty exists
where the information available to the taxpayer does not establish the capability of developing or
improving the product, the method of doing so, or the appropriate design of the product.

400.2 A domestic research expenditure is a research expenditure attributable to research conducted
within the country. The taxpayer deducts a domestic research expenditure in full in the year it is
incurred.

400.3 A research expenditure attributable to research conducted outside the country is a foreign
research expenditure. Rule 410 governs it. This rule does not reach it and it is not deductible
when incurred.

400.4 WHAT WITHIN THE COUNTRY MEANS FOR THIS RULE. Research is conducted within the country where it
is conducted in any of the fifty states, in the federal district, or in any commonwealth, possession
or territory named in the schedule to this rule. Research conducted anywhere else is conducted
outside the country.

SCHEDULE TO RULE 400. The commonwealths, possessions and territories treated as within the country
for 400.4: the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, Guam, the United States Virgin Islands, American Samoa,
and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands. Research conducted in any of these is
conducted within the country and is not foreign research.

400.5 WHAT A RESEARCH EXPENDITURE INCLUDES. A research expenditure includes the wages of employees
engaged in research, the cost of supplies consumed in research, a reasonable allowance for
depreciation on equipment used in research, and amounts paid to another person to perform research
on the taxpayer's behalf. Where an amount is paid to another person to perform research on the
taxpayer's behalf, the whole of that amount is a research expenditure under this rule. No
percentage or haircut is applied to it by this rule.

400.6 SOFTWARE. Amounts paid or incurred for the development of software are research expenditures
for this rule, whether the software is developed for sale or licence to others or for the
taxpayer's own internal use. Internal use makes no difference to this rule. Where the software
development is conducted is what fixes whether it is domestic or foreign under 400.4.

400.7 WHAT THIS RULE DOES NOT REACH. The following are not research expenditures and this rule does
not reach them at all:

(a) ordinary testing or inspection of materials or products for quality control;
(b) efficiency surveys, management studies, market research, market testing and consumer surveys;
(c) advertising and promotion;
(d) the acquisition of another person's patent, model, production process or invention;
(e) research in connection with literary, historical or similar projects;
(f) the cost of acquiring land, or of acquiring property of a character subject to the allowance for
depreciation. A reasonable allowance for depreciation on that property, where the property is used
in research, is still a research expenditure under 400.5. The purchase is out, the depreciation is
in, and both can be true of the same asset.

400.8 THE QUALITY-CONTROL LINE, AND IT IS A FINE ONE. Testing is ordinary testing for quality
control under 400.7(a) where it is testing to determine whether a unit that has been produced
conforms to a design that has already been settled on.

400.9 A cost this rule does not reach is not a domestic research expenditure and it is not a foreign
research expenditure. Where it qualifies on its own terms as an ordinary and necessary business
expense, it is deducted as one in the year it is incurred. Where it is a capital cost on its own
terms, it is capitalised. Either way it is outside this rule, it is outside Rule 410, and it is not
carried into any figure this pack or the circular defines by reference to a research expenditure.

400.10 FUNDING MAKES NO DIFFERENCE TO THIS RULE. A research expenditure is a research expenditure
under this rule whether the taxpayer bears the cost itself or is paid, reimbursed or funded for it
by a grant, a contract or another person. What another person's funding does is Rule 520's business
and not this rule's.

RULE 410. FOREIGN RESEARCH EXPENDITURE, AND THE AMORTISATION.

410.1 A foreign research expenditure is charged to capital account and amortised rateably over
fifteen years. It is not deductible in the year it is incurred and no part of it may be deducted
under Rule 400. The whole of the expenditure is amortised, including the whole of any amount paid
to another person to perform the research on the taxpayer's behalf. No percentage or haircut is
applied to it by this rule.

410.2 THE FIFTEEN YEARS RUN WHATEVER HAPPENS TO THE RESEARCH. Where the property with respect to
which a foreign research expenditure was incurred is disposed of, retired or abandoned during the
fifteen year period, no deduction is allowed on account of that disposition, retirement or
abandonment, and the amortisation continues over the remainder of the period as if the disposition,
retirement or abandonment had not happened. Shelving the work, deleting the code, stopping the
project and abandoning the results are all covered by this and none of them accelerates anything.

410.3 THE MIDPOINT CONVENTION. An expenditure incurred at any point in a year is treated as incurred
at the midpoint of that year. A full year of amortisation is one fifteenth of the expenditure. The
year the expenditure is incurred carries half of a full year, being one thirtieth of the
expenditure, and each of the fourteen following years carries a full one fifteenth, and the
sixteenth year carries the remaining half.

RULE 520. THE RESEARCH CREDIT, AND WHAT QUALIFIES FOR IT.

520.1 This rule is a separate rule from Rule 400 and it has its own test. A cost being a research
expenditure under Rule 400 does not make it a qualifying cost for this rule. A cost failing this
rule does not on that account fail Rule 400. Every cost is sorted under Rule 400 and under this rule
separately, and the two answers do not have to agree.

520.2 THE FOUR-PART TEST. An activity is qualified research for this rule only where all four of
these hold:

(a) the expenditure on it is a research expenditure under Rule 400;
(b) it is undertaken to discover information that is technological in nature;
(c) its application is intended to be useful in the development of a new or improved business
component of the taxpayer; and
(d) substantially all of its activities constitute elements of a process of experimentation relating
to a new or improved function, performance, reliability or quality.

520.3 WHERE THE RESEARCH IS CONDUCTED. Only research conducted within the country is qualified
research for this rule, and within the country here has exactly the meaning it has in 400.4 and the
schedule to Rule 400. Research conducted outside the country is not qualified research and no part
of its cost enters the credit base, whatever Rule 410 does with it.

520.4 THE QUALIFYING COSTS, AND THERE ARE ONLY THREE KINDS.

(a) WAGES. Wages paid to an employee for qualified services, being services engaging in qualified
research, or directly supervising or directly supporting it. The whole of the qualifying wage enters
the credit base.

(b) SUPPLIES. The cost of supplies used in the conduct of qualified research. Supplies means
tangible property other than land, and other than property of a character subject to the allowance
for depreciation. Depreciation on equipment is not a supply cost. No part of a depreciation
allowance enters the credit base under this paragraph or under any other paragraph of this rule,
whatever Rule 400.5 does with it.

(c) CONTRACT RESEARCH. SIXTY-FIVE PER CENT of any amount paid to a person other than an employee of
the taxpayer for qualified research performed on the taxpayer's behalf. The remaining thirty-five
per cent does not enter the credit base. This percentage applies whatever the contract says,
whatever the invoice totals, and whatever Rule 400.5 allows as a deduction on the same amount.

520.5 WHAT THIS RULE EXCLUDES EVEN WHERE 520.2 WOULD OTHERWISE BE MET. None of the following is
qualified research and no part of its cost enters the credit base:

(a) research conducted after the beginning of commercial production of the business component that
the research relates to. Research relating to a new or improved business component is not conducted
after the beginning of commercial production of that component merely because some other component
of the taxpayer is already in commercial production. The question is whether the component the
research relates to is itself already in commercial production;
(b) adaptation of an existing business component to a particular customer's requirement or need;
(c) duplication of an existing business component;
(d) surveys, studies, market research, market testing and routine data collection;
(e) routine or ordinary testing or inspection for quality control. The line between quality-control
testing and other testing is the same line 400.8 draws, and it is drawn the same way here;
(f) research conducted outside the country, per 520.3;
(g) research in the social sciences, the arts or the humanities;
(h) research funded by any grant, by any contract, or by any other person. Research is funded for
this paragraph where another person pays or reimburses the taxpayer's costs of it, whatever Rule
400.10 does with the same cost.

520.6 THE CREDIT FIGURE. The credit for the year is TWENTY PER CENT of the credit base. The credit
base is the sum of the qualifying wages under 520.4(a), the qualifying supply costs under 520.4(b),
and sixty-five per cent of the qualifying contract research payments under 520.4(c).

RULE 530. THE CREDIT REDUCTION, AND WHAT IT REDUCES.

530.1 Where the taxpayer claims the credit under Rule 520, the taxpayer's domestic research
expenditure deduction under Rule 400 is reduced by the full amount of the credit claimed. The
reduction is dollar for dollar and it is not scaled, prorated or capped.

530.2 WHAT THE REDUCTION RUNS AGAINST, AND THIS IS THE WHOLE OF IT. The reduction runs against the
deduction allowed under Rule 400 for the year's domestic research expenditures, and against nothing
else. It does not reduce the Rule 410 amortisation. It does not reduce an ordinary and necessary
business expense deducted under Rule 400.9. It does not reduce a deduction taken under any part of
the circular. A cost that Rule 400 does not reach is not in the base the reduction runs against,
whatever else is true of that cost.

530.3 THE ORDER. Work out the Rule 400 deduction first, work out the Rule 520 credit second, then
apply the reduction. The reduced Rule 400 deduction is the figure that goes on the return."

"ELECTIONS PROCEDURE CIRCULAR 7. Issued for the first returns filed under Rule 400. Fictional
procedure, fictional numbers, invented for testing. This circular is not a citation to anything. This
circular and the rules pack are the whole of the law for this exercise and nothing outside them

PART 1. WHO THIS REACHES.

1.1 A taxpayer is a small taxpayer for this circular where its average annual gross receipts for the
three tax years ending with 2024 do not exceed $31,000,000.

1.2 A taxpayer that is not a small taxpayer cannot make the election in Part 2. A small taxpayer can
make it, subject to Part 2's own window. Being a small taxpayer is what opens the door in Part 2. It
is not what holds the door open, and a taxpayer that is comfortably inside the receipts threshold has
established nothing about whether its window is still there.

PART 2. ELECTION A. THE RETROACTIVE ELECTION FOR 2022, 2023 AND 2024.

2.1 A small taxpayer may elect to treat its domestic research expenditures for the 2022, 2023 and
2024 years as deductible in the year incurred rather than capitalised over five years, and to recover
the difference. The election is made by filing an amended return for EVERY one of those years in
which the taxpayer had domestic research expenditures. An election for fewer than all of those years
is not an election under this part and will not be given effect.

2.2 THE WINDOW. The amended return for a year must be filed by the EARLIER of:

(a) 6 July 2026; or

(b) the last day of the three year period beginning on the day the taxpayer's original return for
that year was actually filed.

2.3 There is no extension of either date in 2.2 and there is no relief for a late election under this
part. Where the window has closed for any one of the three years, the election under this part cannot
be made at all, because 2.1 requires all of them.

2.4 WORKED EXAMPLE 1. A calendar year small taxpayer filed its original 2022 return on 15 October
2023, which was its extended due date. The three year period in 2.2(b) begins on 15 October 2023 and
its last day is 14 October 2026. That is later than 6 July 2026, so 2.2(a) is the earlier of the two
and the window for 2022 closed on 6 July 2026.

2.5 WORKED EXAMPLE 2, AND IT IS THE ONE PEOPLE MISS. A calendar year small taxpayer filed its
original 2022 return early, on 14 February 2023, without waiting for the due date. The three year
period in 2.2(b) begins on 14 February 2023 and its last day is 13 February 2026. That is EARLIER
than 6 July 2026, so 2.2(b) is the earlier of the two and the window for 2022 closed on 13 February
2026, nearly five months before the date in 2.2(a). Filing early shortened the window. The day the
return was ACTUALLY FILED is what starts the three year period. The due date does not start it, an
extension of the due date does not start it, and the date in 2.2(a) does not rescue a year whose own
period has already run out.

2.6 Each of the three years has its own window under 2.2 and each is worked out on its own facts.
Because 2.1 requires all three years, the election under this part is unavailable from the moment the
earliest of the three windows closes.

PART 3. ELECTION B. THE ACCELERATION ELECTION ON THE 2025 RETURN.

3.1 A taxpayer that holds an unamortised balance of DOMESTIC research expenditures capitalised under
the law as it stood before Rule 400 may elect to deduct that remaining balance, either in full on its
2025 return or rateably over its 2025 and 2026 returns.

3.2 THE SCOPE, AND IT IS NARROW. This election reaches the unamortised balance of DOMESTIC research
expenditures and nothing else. It does not reach a balance of foreign research expenditures, whether
that balance was capitalised under Rule 410 or under the law as it stood before Rule 410. No balance
of foreign research expenditures may be accelerated, shortened, written off or recovered under this
circular or under any other provision. Rule 410.2 governs those balances and it governs them whatever
happens to the research.

3.3 THE WINDOW. The election is made by attaching a statement to the taxpayer's timely filed 2025
return, including extensions. For a calendar year taxpayer on extension that date is 15 October 2026.
The election is open until that date and it is not available after it.

3.4 A deduction taken under this part is taken under this circular. It is not a Rule 400 deduction,
it does not enter the Rule 520 credit base, and the Rule 530 reduction does not run against it.

3.5 A taxpayer that does not make this election continues to amortise the balance over the period
that was already running on it. Rule 400 does not reach an expenditure that was incurred before the
first year Rule 400 applies to.

3.6 ELECTION A AND ELECTION B ARE SEPARATE. Neither is a condition of the other. The closing of the
window in Part 2 does not close the window in Part 3, and it does not open it either. Nothing in Part
2 is required before Part 3 can be used."
