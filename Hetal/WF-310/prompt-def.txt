Working as of Wednesday 29 July 2026, timezone Asia/Kolkata. I lead retrieval quality for our support RAG
assistant. Every so often I pull the last stretch of production queries and check how much our BM25 and
dense retrievers actually overlap versus cover different ground, because I keep hearing from engineering
that "dense retrieval should be enough now" and I don't think the query log backs that up.

Everything you need is in the GitHub repo "support-rag-eval": "corpus.md" has the document chunks our RAG
system can retrieve from, "query_log.md" has the production queries with their confirmed ground-truth
chunk(s) (some queries have two acceptable chunks, some are marked disputed between two candidates, some
have no acceptable chunk in the corpus at all) and the exact top-10 ranked results our BM25 retriever and
our dense retriever already returned in production for each one, and "recipe.md" has the exact retrieval
parameters used to generate those rankings.

For every query, work out whether BM25 alone found an acceptable chunk in its top 5, whether dense alone
did, and put the query into exactly one bucket based purely on retrieval outcome: exact-match if only BM25
found it, semantic if only dense found it, mixed if both found it, if neither found it alone but a fused
ranking does, or if the query has two acceptable chunks and each retriever independently found a different
one of the two, and no-coverage if nothing finds it at all, even fused. The no-coverage bucket is a
retrieval-miss bucket only: it applies whenever BM25, dense, and the optimal fused ranking all miss in the
top 5, regardless of whether an acceptable chunk actually exists in the corpus. For a disputed-ground-truth
query, count a hit for a method if either of the two disputed candidate chunks appears in that method's top
5, and flag these queries as disputed in your output rather than silently resolving the dispute yourself.
Treat every query as its own row even if it looks like a paraphrase of another query in the log; do not
merge or deduplicate them. Do not decide any bucket from how the query reads; decide it from what each
retriever actually returned for that specific query.

Separately from that bucket, set a corpus-gap flag to yes only when "query_log.md" itself records that no
acceptable ground-truth chunk exists anywhere in the corpus for that query. This flag is about whether the
content exists at all, not about whether retrieval found it, so it does not come from your BM25/dense/fused
computation. Every corpus-gap query will also land in the no-coverage bucket, since no retriever can hit a
chunk that was never in the corpus, but not every no-coverage query is a corpus-gap: some no-coverage
queries have an acceptable chunk in the corpus that BM25, dense, and the fused ranking all happened to miss
in their top 5, and that is a retrieval miss, not a corpus gap. Keep the two concepts distinct in your
output rather than treating no-coverage and corpus-gap as synonyms.

Then implement Reciprocal Rank Fusion yourself and sweep it: for a given fusion weight α, a chunk's fused
score is α × 1/(k + its BM25 rank) + (1 − α) × 1/(k + its dense rank), using the k value from "recipe.md"
and 0 for a side the chunk doesn't appear in at all. Test α at every step of 0.1 from 0.0 to 1.0 inclusive,
and for each value work out the fused top 5 per query and the overall Recall@5 across the whole query log.
Report whichever α gives the highest overall Recall@5 as the optimal fusion weight, and use that α's fused
rankings to decide the mixed bucket above and to fill in each query's hybrid hit/miss.

Work out BM25-only Recall@5, dense-only Recall@5, and hybrid Recall@5 at the optimal α, all across the full
query log, and check whether hybrid clears at least a 39% relative improvement over dense-only. State the
actual percentage improvement you get, not just whether it clears the bar.
