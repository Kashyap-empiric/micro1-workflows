For each opportunity, resolve every Touchpoint Click ID against the campaign data (by Click ID) and the GA4 session data (by Click ID, falling back to Contact ID if the Click ID is missing from a session row), and reconstruct an ordered timeline of every marketing touch that preceded it, earliest first. If a Click ID can't be resolved to any campaign row, drop it from the timeline and record it under Data Quality Notes instead of guessing at its source. If an opportunity ends up with no resolved campaign touches at all, exclude it from attribution calculations, audience overlap calculations, and any validation that requires attribution totals, and record it under Data Quality Notes.

Attribution runs on a position-based (U-shaped) model instead of last-click: 40% credit to the first touch in the timeline, 40% to the last touch, and the remaining 20% split evenly across whatever touches fall in between. A single-touch opportunity gets 100% on that touch. A two-touch opportunity splits 50/50 between first and last, since there's no middle share to allocate.

For each Platform + Campaign Type combination, the report needs: Attributed Conversions (sum of attribution percentages across every opportunity that touched it, regardless of stage); Attributed Pipeline (sum of attribution % × Amount across opportunities still open in Discovery, Demo Scheduled, or Proposal); Attributed Revenue (sum of attribution % × Amount across Closed Won opportunities only); and True CAC (Spend ÷ Attributed Conversions, or "N/A - no attributed conversions" when that's zero). The blended average True CAC is total spend across all combinations divided by their total Attributed Conversions, or "N/A" if every combination has zero attributed conversions, in which case only the ₹50,000-spend-with-zero-conversions underperformance rule below still applies.

The report also needs a last-click comparison: recompute the same table crediting 100% to only the final touch of each timeline (or the sole touch, if there's only one), and compare last-click CAC to true CAC per combination. A combination is "materially over-credited" when last-click CAC is more than 20% lower than true CAC, and "materially under-credited" when last-click CAC is more than 20% higher, including cases where last-click CAC comes out "N/A" while true CAC is a real number, which counts as the maximum possible under-crediting.

For every opportunity with two or more touches, work out whether those touches span more than one platform. Calculate that percentage using all opportunities with at least one resolved marketing touch as the denominator, and list the platform pairings involved (for example, Google Ads + LinkedIn Ads) ranked by how often they co-occur.
